1. Start by reviewing the data.gov home page. What is this?

Data.gov is the U.S. government's website for open data. It gives the public access to hundreds of thousands of datasets from federal, state, and local government agencies. People can use these datasets for research, analysis, and learning about different topics.

2. Go to Most Viewed Datasets. How many datasets are listed?

Data.gov currently provides access to about 590,000 datasets.

3. Find the Topic Category for Human Health. Review the range of data sources
available for this topic. Why do you think these were tagged with the Human Health
category?

The Human Health category includes datasets related to nutrition, food access, environmental health, and other topics that affect people's well-being. They are tagged as Human Health because the information can help researchers and policymakers better understand factors that influence public health.

4. Go to the Food Price Outlook. Review the catalog page for this dataset. How can you
navigate to additional datasets from the USDA?

On the Food Price Outlook page, you can find more USDA datasets by clicking on the USDA organization links, exploring related datasets, or searching other USDA resources within Data.gov

5. Go back to the data.gov home page. This time, click on Datasets by Organization.

This section lets you browse datasets based on the government agency or organization that published them. It makes it easier to find data from a specific source.

6. Try searching for your city, county, and state. What organizations are returned?

The organizations returned will depend on the location you search. For example, you may see state government agencies, county departments, city governments, or local data portals associated with that area.

7. Search for USDA, and click into the result. Within the USDA datasets, search for food
composition. Notice that the top three results are all datasets concerning nutrition
information. Try searching the USDA page for nutrition. How does that affect your
results?

a) One of the tricky things about working with data is that sometimes the terminology
used by a data source is not obvious. You may need to dig around and be creative
to find what you’re looking for.

This shows that finding data often requires trying different search terms. A dataset may contain the information you need, but it might be labeled using different words than you expect, so being flexible with keywords can help you find more useful results.

8. Go back to the results for food composition, and click on FoodData Central. Review
the catalog page for this data source, then click on the button for Visit Page. Take
some time to explore the FoodData Central website.

FoodData Central is a USDA database that provides detailed information about foods and their nutritional content. It allows users to search for foods, view nutrient information, download datasets, and access data through an API

a) Try searching for butter, or any of your favorite foods. What kind of results do you
get? What happens when you click on a result?

When you search for a food such as butter, you get a list of matching foods along with nutrition information. Clicking a result opens a detailed page that shows nutrients, serving sizes, calories, and other information about that food

b) Go back to the home page. Find Foundation Foods, and click on Downloads. What
kind of data can you find on this page? How big are these files? Where can you find
more information on the data types available

The Foundation Foods download section contains food composition data, nutrient values, metadata, and supporting documentation. The files can be quite large, ranging from several megabytes to much larger datasets. More information about the different data types is available in the documentation and About Us sections of FoodData Central.
